# UNDO — Game Vision

## One-sentence pitch

A first-person puzzle game where the player cannot rewind time or directly operate the world; they can only suppress the direct state change caused by a recorded past event, then exploit the contradictions left behind.

## Player fantasy

The player is not a wizard, hacker, combatant, or omnipotent time traveler. The fantasy is **causal literacy**: observe a functioning environment, understand why it reached its current state, remove one link, and predict what the world will do next.

The desired thought process is:

1. Observe a routine.
2. Identify a causal chain.
3. Predict the consequence of removing one recorded action.
4. Suppress that event.
5. Watch people or machines react to the contradiction.
6. Exploit the new event created by that reaction.
7. Release or replace corrections when needed.

## Genre and scope

- First-person causal puzzle
- Narrative mystery
- Approximately 4–6 hours
- Single continuous facility
- Five acts
- Sixteen major puzzle spaces
- No combat
- No inventory
- No skill tree
- No crafting
- No open world
- No procedural generation
- No multiplayer
- No dialogue-choice system
- No reflex-heavy platforming

## Core dramatic idea

The game begins by teaching the player that recorded actions can be undone. Its narrative eventually confronts the player with an event that cannot be undone because the crucial action never happened.

Internal thematic statement:

> Actions can be undone. Inaction cannot.

This statement should be expressed through mechanics, not explanatory monologue.

## Experience goals

The player should frequently experience:
- curiosity while watching routines,
- comprehension when a causal relationship becomes clear,
- a clean "of course" realization when a solution is understood,
- satisfaction from creating logically impossible but rule-consistent states,
- unease as institutional records become inconsistent,
- restraint rather than melodrama in the narrative conclusion.

## Non-goals

UNDO is not:
- a conventional time-rewind game,
- a physics rewind simulator,
- a horror game,
- a cyberpunk game,
- an AI-themed game,
- a supernatural ghost story,
- an action stealth game,
- a lore-collection game,
- a game about choosing a "good" or "bad" ending from a menu.

## Production principle

Mechanics are validated before full content production.

Required gates:
1. Basic event suppression feels legible.
2. Consequence Persistence produces an enjoyable realization.
3. NPC reactions are predictable and exploitable.
4. A polished vertical slice proves the complete experience.

If the second gate fails, the project should be redesigned before significant art or narrative production.
